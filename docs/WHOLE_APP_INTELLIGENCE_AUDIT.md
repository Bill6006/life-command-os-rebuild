## 1. Executive judgement

This app is real. It decides, it abstains, it explains, it learns, it says when it does not
know, and several of its sentences are better than anything a productivity app writes. The
association engine (`association.ts`) is genuinely good statistics with genuinely careful
language. The recovery-versus-ambition reasoning is emergent rather than hardcoded. The
evidence panel is honest.

It is also, at this moment, an **evening app with a whole-life vocabulary bolted on**.

Three structural facts explain almost every finding below:

1. **The moment is a block, and the block is assumed to be the evening.** The situation
   summariser is block-aware; the limiter, the buttons, the question options, the learning
   labels, the evidence panel and the explanation templates are not. Below noon the app is
   not merely mis-worded — it has *no recovery move in existence* and can tell a man who is
   nine hours short of sleep to study.
2. **Four of eleven domains are write-only.** `emotionalState`, `faithPractice`,
   `privatePattern` and `custodyArrangement` are stored, shown on their pages, counted by
   coverage — and read by nothing in `src/intelligence/`. They cannot influence a decision,
   and because `mattersToOwner` reads importance off history they also cannot become stale,
   so nothing ever asks about them either.
3. **There is no strategy object.** Every recommendation is computed from scratch. Nothing
   connects yesterday to today, nothing sequences, nothing recognises that an approach is
   failing, and `arbitrate()` cannot see what it said an hour ago. Nine consecutive evenings
   with his daughter produce the same nine sentences.

The single most important consequence for the owner: **the app has the least to say at the
moments that matter most** — the morning, and the three evenings a month his daughter is away.

## A. Temporal and contextual reasoning

### What the system believes "now" means

`now` is an `Instant` plus a `TimeZoneId`. Everything owner-facing is derived from it:
`localDayIdAt`, `localWeekIdAt`, and `blockOfLocalTime` (`src/domain/time.ts:435`), which cuts
the day into five blocks — late-night `<04:00`, early-morning `04:00–07:00`, morning
`07:00–12:00`, afternoon `12:00–18:00`, evening `18:00–22:00`, late-night `22:00–24:00`.

That belief lives in `Situation.block`, and it is correct. The problem is how few things read it.

### A.3 — AUD-0003

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **ID** | AUD-0003 |
| **Title** | Before noon there is no recovery move in existence, so the app names a nine-hour sleep debt and prescribes a study session |
| **Type** | **BUG** — and the most damaging finding in this audit |
| **Current behaviour** | `sleepCandidates` returns `[]` outright in the morning and early-morning (`candidates.ts:199`), and every recovery verb refuses those blocks in its profile (`protect-sleep`, `wind-down`, `recover` all `refuses: ['morning','early-morning']`; `ease-off` suits `afternoon` only — `moves.ts:181-231`). So on a morning where `strain` is severe, the only candidates generated are career moves. The constraint filter then rejects the two heaviest of them as `too-strained`, and the survivor is recommended. |
| **Problem** | The screen names the limiter and then contradicts it. The app is telling a man it has just told is nine hours short of rest to go and study. This is scenario **G-005 failing before noon** — "career does not win automatically when rest is what is short" holds in the evening and does not hold in the morning. It is also, precisely, the class the defect ledger already names in **DEF-0016**: *"a filter with no fallback"*. DEF-0016 fixed the afternoon by adding `ease-off`, and its regression sweeps "every half hour **from noon to midnight**". The morning was never swept. |
| **Owner-facing example** | "Three broken nights, and a deadline", clock at **10:00 Tuesday**. Now, verbatim: *"Tuesday morning, 9 hours short on sleep."* / **"RECALL PRACTICE — Spend 10 minutes recalling subnetting before you reopen your notes."** / *"Subnetting went badly yesterday — the /26 boundaries went wrong twice. This week is about the CCNA push."* / *"What is in the way — About 9 hours short of rest over the last few nights."* The probe for the same instant: `Moves considered: 3` — all three career; `Ruled out: 2` — both `too-strained — too much to ask of tonight`. At 06:30 the same history gives the same career move. At 14:00 the recovery move appears and wins at 0.334 against 0.198. |
| **Evidence** | `src/intelligence/candidates.ts:199`; `src/intelligence/moves.ts:181-231`; `docs/DEFECT_LEDGER.md` DEF-0016 (class and sweep bounds). Live reproduction above; Stage 1 note **S1-J**. |
| **Likely root cause** | The early return at `candidates.ts:199` is honest about the verb table — there is no morning-shaped recovery verb, so proposing one would only be refused. The defect is that nothing was put in its place, and the arbiter is left choosing among effortful moves while the limiter says rest. |
| **Recommended behaviour** | Add a morning-shaped recovery verb — working name `lighten-the-day` — that suits `early-morning` and `morning` and refuses the rest, mirroring `ease-off`. Its content is well supported: after chronic restriction, deficits accumulate and recovery takes several unrestricted nights, and self-rated sleepiness under-reports the impairment (Van Dongen et al., *Sleep* 26(2):117–126, 2003). So the honest morning move is not "sleep more now" — it is *don't put the hard thing in today, and protect tonight*: "Keep today light — move the subnetting session to tomorrow and get to bed early." Additionally, make the general rule explicit and testable: **when the engine can name a limiter, at least one candidate must address it, in every block.** |
| **Benefit** | The app stops arguing with itself on exactly the mornings it is most needed, and G-005 becomes true for the whole day rather than for the half of it that was tested. |
| **Implementation scope** | `recommendation.ts` (one verb + templates), `moves.ts` (one profile), `candidates.ts` (replace the early return with the block-appropriate verb, as DEF-0016 did for the afternoon), `explain.ts` (a `deficit` branch that reads in the morning). |
| **Risks** | A new verb changes what the engine can suggest — the same reason DEF-0016 was deferred out of Phase 2. It needs its own outcome aspects and an outcome window; judging "did you keep today light?" belongs the following morning, not in twenty minutes. There is a modest risk of it out-competing genuinely fine mornings; gate it on `strain !== 'none'` exactly as the existing sleep generator does. |
| **Schema / data impact** | One new `ActionVerb` value. `ACTION_VERBS` is a closed union stamped into stored `action-recommendation` records, so this is an additive schema change with no migration, but old records must keep parsing. |
| **Tests required** | Extend `tests/synthetic/recovery-has-somewhere-to-go.test.ts` to sweep **midnight to midnight** rather than noon to midnight. Add the invariant: for every scenario × block, if `situation.limiter` is `recovery` or `capacity`, at least one candidate's domain matches the limiter's domain. Assert the 10:00 reproduction flips. |
| **Priority** | **P0** |
| **Timing** | **BEFORE PHASE 9 (Phase 8.5)** |

### A.4 — AUD-0004

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **ID** | AUD-0004 |
| **Title** | The day blocks are clock arithmetic, not this owner's day; the app knows nothing about what is coming |
| **Type** | **INTELLIGENCE** |
| **Current behaviour** | Five fixed blocks from wall-clock minutes (`time.ts:435`). "Morning" spans 07:00–12:00; "afternoon" spans 12:00–18:00. There is no representation of school, work, custody handover, bedtime, an appointment, or anything scheduled. The only forward-looking quantity in the whole engine is `career.usable-time-tonight`, a single self-reported number. Nothing asks or infers what is coming later today, tomorrow, or this weekend. |

| **Likely root cause** | The time architecture (plan section 15) models *identity* — instants, local days, local weeks, freshness windows — thoroughly and correctly. It does not model *commitments*. There is no `obligation` record kind and no recurring-rhythm concept, so nothing downstream can reason about them. |
| **Recommended behaviour** | Two additions, in this order. **(a) A `commitment-window` record** — a named obligation with a local-day/time span and an optional recurrence (weekdays, school term). Seed it from two questions the owner would answer once ("What time does Adaya's school day start and end?", "What are your working hours?"). The situation then carries `minutesUntilNextObligation` alongside `usableMinutes`, and `time-fit` and `opportunity-cost` read it. **(b) Make `hold` reachable**: when the best candidate's `context-fit` is materially higher in a later block today, the arbiter may return `hold` with the block named — *"Not now. | **Implementation scope** | `src/domain/records.ts` (new record kind), `concepts.ts`, `situation.ts` (assemble the next obligation), `evaluate.ts` (`time-fit`, `opportunity-cost`), `arbitrate.ts` (a defer path), `questions.ts` (two durable questions), a Life surface to correct them. | **Tests required** | Adversarial: a school-morning history where the same move is right at 10:00 and wrong at 07:15. A `hold` test asserting the deferral names a real later block and that `hold` cannot win when no later block scores higher. Contract test that a `commitment-window` survives backup → restore. |
| **Priority** | **P1** |
| **Timing** | **PHASE 10** (too large for 8.5; AUD-0003 and AUD-0001 are the parts that cannot wait) |

### A.5 — AUD-0005

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **ID** | AUD-0005 |
| **Title** | Freshness is a rolling countdown rather than a validity window, so last night's sleep expires mid-morning |
| **Type** | **BUG** |
| **Current behaviour** | Every concept carries a `FreshnessHorizon` measured as a duration from the reading. A `sleep.hours-last-night` reading recorded on Tuesday evening is `known` at 06:30 Wednesday and `stale` at 10:00 Wednesday. Meanwhile `home.friction` — "the kitchen table is buried again" — remains `known` all day. |
| **Problem** | "Hours slept last night" is a fact about a *specific night*. It does not become less true at ten in the morning; it stops being true when he next sleeps. Conversely, "the kitchen table is buried" is one of the most perishable facts in the model — he may have cleared it — and it is the one that persists. The windows are semantically backwards in at least this pair, and the consequence is that the app loses its best morning fact at the exact hour it most needs it (see AUD-0003). |
| **Owner-facing example** | "A week pointed at the house": at 06:30 the situation line reads *"Wednesday early morning, 8 hours of sleep, Adaya is here."*; at 10:00 the same day it reads *"Wednesday morning, Adaya is here."* The eight hours have vanished from the app's picture of the same morning. |
| **Evidence** | Probe fact list captured at both hours (Stage 1 note **S1-B**): `Hours slept last night · known` → `· stale`, same value, same night. `src/domain/windows.ts` (`FreshnessHorizon`), `src/memory/facts.ts`. |
| **Likely root cause** | `FreshnessHorizon` has two units — `local-days` and `ms` — and both are *elapsed-time* measures. There is no unit meaning "valid for the local day it describes" or "valid until the next reading of this concept". |
| **Recommended behaviour** | Add a third horizon unit, `until-next-of-kind` / `this-local-day`, and assign it per concept: `sleepHours` and `sleepQuality` are valid for the local day that follows the night they describe; `usableTimeTonight` is valid within its block and expires at the block boundary (not after N hours); `homeFriction` gets a *shorter* window than it has, or is superseded by any `reset-space` completion. This is a per-concept semantic decision, not a global constant. |
| **Benefit** | The app stops forgetting facts that are still true and stops asserting facts that have stopped being true — both are section 63's rule, applied to the freshness model rather than to a surface. |
| **Implementation scope** | `src/domain/windows.ts` (one new unit), `src/memory/facts.ts` (resolution), the concept registry entries, and `association.ts:178` `maxGapFor` which switches on the unit. |
| **Risks** | Changing a window changes what the guide asks and what the evaluator's `uncertainty` dimension penalises, so scenario outputs will move. Every change must be justified per concept in the decision log; a blanket widening would breach section 63 in the other direction. |
| **Schema / data impact** | No stored-record change; the horizon lives in the registry, not in records. |
| **Tests required** | Per-concept validity tests: a sleep reading taken at 22:00 is `known` at 09:00 the next day and `stale` after the next night's reading exists or after the following night has passed. A usable-time reading is `stale` in the next block. Assert the 06:30 → 10:00 reproduction no longer drops the sleep fact. |
| **Priority** | **P1** |
| **Timing** | **BEFORE PHASE 9 (Phase 8.5)** — AUD-0003 depends on the morning keeping its sleep fact |

### A.6 — AUD-0006

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **ID** | AUD-0006 |
| **Title** | The app's only model of available time is a career-namespaced concept called "usable time tonight", filed under the direction domain |
| **Type** | **ARCHITECTURE** |

## B. Every domain, interrogated

The eleven domains are enumerated from `src/domain/domains.ts` and confirmed on the Life
screen: health, sleep, fatherhood, career, money, social, emotional, faith, home, private,
direction. Ten baseline pages; Health & Physical Capacity and Sleep & Recovery deliberately
share one (D-078).

### B.0 The structural fact underneath every domain finding

The concept registry holds 15 owner concepts. `assembleSituation` reads **nine** of them:
`sleepHours`, `sleepQuality`, `energy`, `soreness`, `usableTimeTonight`, `childPresent`,
`socialEnergy`, `homeFriction`, `learningTopic`, plus `weeklyFocus` for direction
(`situation.ts:575-596`). `cashBuffer` is read by the money generator directly off
`situation.view.facts` (`candidates.ts:523`), bypassing the situation — so it decides
something but is absent from the trace's "Facts considered".

**`emotionalState`, `faithPractice`, `privatePattern` and `custodyArrangement` are referenced
nowhere in `src/intelligence/`.** Verified: their only references outside the registry are in
`src/synthetic/scenarios.ts` (which writes them) and `src/legacy/mapping.ts` (which maps
faith practice in). They are stored, projected, rendered on a domain page, and counted by the
coverage engine — and no path exists from any of them to `decide()`.

Eight generators exist, covering seven domains plus coverage: sleep, career, fatherhood,
home, social, health, money, coverage (`candidates.ts:632-644`). The coverage fallback
(`REFRESHING_MOVE`, `:566`) covers only home, career and money.

### Domain-by-domain

**Health & Physical Capacity.** *Knows:* energy (scale 1–5) and soreness (scale), both
self-reported or inferred. *Can recommend:* one verb, `move`. *A thoughtful person would
notice:* whether he has eaten, whether he is ill, whether the soreness is new or the same
shoulder as last month, whether a walk is what he actually enjoys. *Guessing:* `energy` is
inferred at 50% confidence and then described as "There is enough in the tank". *Language:*
good — "There is enough in the tank for a walk" is the right register. *Biggest gap:* one
verb for an entire body. *Progress:* activity only; nothing tracks capacity trending up.

**Sleep & Recovery.** *Knows:* hours last night, quality last night, and a derived `strain`
and `sleepDebtHours` across recent nights. This is the best-modelled domain in the app and
the derivation is genuinely good. *Can recommend:* four verbs — `protect-sleep`, `wind-down`,
`recover`, `ease-off` — none of which exists before noon (AUD-0003). *Would notice:* that
three bad nights in a row is a pattern with a cause; that recovery takes more than one night.
*Language:* "You are 9 hours down over the last 3 nights. Subnetting will still be there
tomorrow." — the best sentence in the app. *Biggest gap:* the recommendation is always a
single night (AUD-0009).

**Fatherhood / Family.** See section C in full.

**Career & Learning.** *Knows:* one current learning topic, active goals, and the sentiment
of the last outcome. *Can recommend:* three verbs — recall practice, review weak topic,
hands-on lab. The verb selection is genuinely intelligent: a *specific* recorded failure
routes to review rather than recall. *Would notice:* that the exam is in eleven weeks and he
has done four sessions; that the same topic has been "current" for four months. *Guessing:*
nothing egregious. *Language:* the best in the app — "Pass the CCNA before the winter — and
subnetting is the weak part." *Biggest gap:* no schedule (AUD-0010). *Progress:* sessions
happened, not whether he knows more.

**Money.** *Knows:* one scalar, `cashBuffer`, plus financial-goal entities. *Can recommend:*
`handle-money-item`, and only when a `financial-goal` entity exists. *Would notice:* that
money stress is showing up as bad sleep. *Language:* untested in the library — no scenario
exercises money at all. *Biggest gap:* it is functionally dormant (AUD-0012). *Progress:* none.

**Social & Relationships.** *Knows:* `socialEnergy`. *Can recommend:* `reach-out`,
`start-conversation`, and only when `socialEnergy >= 0.6` **and** a place or person entity
exists. *Would notice:* who he has not spoken to in three months. *Language:* "Start one real
conversation while you are at the climbing gym" is specific and free of the quota mentality
section 10 forbids — this is right. *Biggest gap:* it fires only when he has already told the
app he feels sociable, which is the opposite of when it would help.

**Emotional Health.** *Knows:* nothing the brain can read. **Write-only.** *Can recommend:*
nothing. *Biggest gap:* total.

**Faith & Meaning.** As above. **Write-only.** The legacy mapper brings
`faith:practice-done` across into `CONCEPT.faithPractice`, which nothing then reads.

**Home & Environment.** *Knows:* `homeFriction`, free text. *Can recommend:* `reset-space`.
*Would notice:* that the kitchen has been the friction for nine months. *Language:* "The
kitchen table is buried again — and it costs you the start of every evening" is asserted as
fact from a stored free-text value plus a canned causal clause (AUD-0028). *Biggest gap:* it
is the domain most likely to win by default on an ordinary evening, because it always has
something to say.

**Private / Sexual Health.** **Write-only.** Section 11 states that "private evidence may
still influence whole-life reasoning when relevant" and gives two examples. Neither is
possible: `privatePattern` reaches no reader. Discretion is well handled — it is off by
default in exports and gated behind a toggle in QA — but discretion without influence is a
page, not a domain.

**Long-Range Direction / Identity.** *Knows:* `weeklyFocus` (an entity reference with a
category) and goal entities. The weekly direction genuinely decides ordinary evenings —
`direction-fit` carries weight 1.8 and scenario G-008 comes out right. Expiry at the local
week boundary works. *Biggest gap:* it has no generator of its own, and long-range goals only
touch daily moves through `goal-fit` matching a domain (AUD-0021).

## C. Fatherhood and Adaya

This is the section the product most needs to get right and the one where the intelligence is
thinnest. The entire developmental model is `src/intelligence/growth.ts` — **238 lines** —
plus one generator branch in `candidates.ts:363-397`.

**What it actually does.** For each `development-skill` entity linked to the child, it counts
episodes of the `growth-opportunity` verb that reached `completed` **and** scored ≥ 0.9
(`GROWTH_CLEARLY`, `growth.ts:41`). At three such occasions (`GROWTH_OCCASIONS = 3`, `:38`)
it offers one sentence beside the decision: *"Adaya has handled ordering her own food 3 times
running. Worth calling that settled?"* → "Yes, she has got this" / "Not yet". Agreeing writes
a `domain-update` carrying a free-text summary; declining writes a `coverage-update`. A skill
whose last occasion is ≥ 14 days old (`GROWTH_EVIDENCE_AGES_AFTER_DAYS`, `:52`) is treated as
having aged, and the candidate's trigger flips from `opportunity-window` to `stale-evidence`.

**Answering the brief's questions directly.**

- *Can it represent a skill that is emerging?* **No.** Occasions below 0.9 are discarded by
  `continue` and contribute nothing. A child with eight partial attempts and three clean ones
  is indistinguishable from one with three clean ones and nothing else.
- *Partial independence — with a familiar adult but not a stranger; at home but not in
  public?* **No.** An occasion records one ordinal and no context. There is no `where`, no
  `with whom`, no `how much help`.
- *"Not ready yet" vs "had an off day" vs "regressed"?* **No.** All three are the same thing:
  an occasion that did not reach 0.9, silently dropped.
- *Does a small run of clean occasions mean she has changed?* Not on this evidence. Behaviour-
  analytic practice treats generalisation as something that must be *programmed and probed*
  across settings, people and time rather than assumed from repetition in one context (Stokes
  & Baer, *Journal of Applied Behavior Analysis* 10(2):349–367, 1977); and where mastery
  criteria have been compared, stringent criteria plus **spaced maintenance probes** predicted
  retention far better than a short consecutive run. Three good occasions in one kind of place
  is the definition of "train and hope".
- *Does anything understand her age, or what is normal for it?* **No.** There is no
  birthdate, no age, and no developmental norm anywhere in the codebase. The CDC's
  *Learn the Signs. Act Early.* checklists define a milestone as something ≥75% of children do
  by a given age, with evidence-informed placement (Zubler et al., *Pediatrics* 149(3), 2022) —
  so a normative anchor exists and is free.
- *Does it risk framing normal development as deficit?* Yes, structurally — see AUD-0016.
- *Does it reduce her to grades, scores or streaks?* **"3 times running"** is a streak, and
  it is the only thing the app ever says about her development.
- *Can it suggest an appropriate next opportunity?* **No.** Mastery ends the story.
- *Does it know what she has mastered, so it stops suggesting it?* **No** — AUD-0015, verified.
- *Does it understand practice spacing?* Partly: the 14-day rule stops it suggesting the same
  thing three nights running. But 14 days is a fixed interval for every skill at every stage.
- *Does it model her interests, temperament, what she enjoys?* **No.** Only skills he entered.
- *Does it reason about where they are?* **No.** There is no location model at all.
- *Does it distinguish time WITH her from time working ON something with her?* **Yes**, and
  well — `time-with` and `growth-opportunity` are separate verbs, and *"Spend the next 30
  minutes with Adaya, phone away"* is a real recommendation the app makes. This is the single
  best product decision in the fatherhood domain and it must be protected.
- *What does it do with years of history about her?* Nothing. Legacy `skill-claim` and
  `milestone-observation` are archived and barred from decisions (AUD-0031).

growth-opportunity, reach-out, start-conversation, reset-space, handle-money-item, move, hold.
Coverage by domain: career 3, sleep 4, fatherhood 2, social 2, home 1, money 1, health 1,
universal 1 (`hold`, ungenerated). Emotional, faith, private and direction: **zero**.

**Outcome horizons.** Two: `same-block` (after N minutes) and `next-morning`
(`moves.ts:45-52`). There is no week horizon, no month horizon, no "did this help over six
weeks" — so the answer to the brief's question is that **this app can never learn that
something helped over six weeks.**

**Plan structure.** None. No prerequisite, no sequence, no follow-up chain, no plan record.
`arbitrate()` receives `(evaluations, situation, ruledOut)` and nothing else — it cannot see
what it chose yesterday, or an hour ago.

## E. Cross-domain intelligence

**Does the app understand that sleep affects everything?** Partly, and this is its best piece
of reasoning. `strain` is derived from recent nights and becomes the `recovery` limiter;
`bottleneck-fit` carries weight 2.5; an effortful move fits badly against a recovery limiter
because of `demand`, not because a rule says sleep beats career. G-005 and its inverse both
come out right in the evening. That is genuine cross-domain intelligence and it should be
protected. It fails before noon (AUD-0003) and it is the *only* cross-domain channel that exists.

**Money stress → emotional state → patience with a child?** Not representable. `cashBuffer`
is not in the situation; `emotionalState` is read by nothing.

**Social energy and fatherhood competing for the same evening?** Only as two candidates
ranked against each other, with no acknowledgement that the trade-off was made.

**Career study vs sleep on a school night?** Handled well in the evening — this is G-005.

**Does the limiter model produce cross-domain reasoning, or pick one domain and silence the
others?** It genuinely reasons: the limiter is a reading of the *situation*, and `findLimiter`
deliberately reads no domain preference. But it does silence — one limiter is chosen from a
fixed priority (recovery → capacity → time → coverage) and only that one is spoken.

## F. Learning, memory and history

**Does it genuinely learn, or apply fixed priors with small adjustments?** Both, honestly
labelled. `moves.ts` holds priors; `learning.ts` shrinks them toward observation by
`n / (n + PATIENCE)` with `PATIENCE = 3` (`learning.ts:58`, `:484`), reliability-weighted per
source and concept (D-059). Twelve occasions pull a prior about 69% of the way. That is a
defensible design and the trace reports it honestly.

**The thresholds.** `PATIENCE = 3`; `RECOGNISABLE = 0.4` (minimum similarity to count);
`recencyFactor = 0.6 + 0.4·e^(−days/60)`, floored at 0.6 so old evidence never vanishes;
`MIN_PAIRS = 4` per side for an association; `MATERIAL_GAP = 0.34`; `MIN_FOR_A_RATE = 4`;
`STALE_BELIEF_DAYS = 60`. These are well chosen. `MIN_PAIRS = 4` *on each side with a
comparison group* is genuinely conservative and the copy that comes out of it is careful.

**Section 16's horizons.** Nine are required — moment, block, today, recent days, recent
weeks, recent months, life season, long-term baseline, all retained history. What exists is
**one continuous decay curve** and one `life-season` insight card that no decision reads.
There is no structure named for a month, a season or a baseline. See AUD-0007.

**Contextual historical comparison** works and is not merely date proximity: `similarity()`
compares block, strain, weekend, child presence and usable time. Five of the ten factors
section 16 lists. See AUD-0007.

**Can it discover a pattern the owner does not already know?** Yes — once. `association.ts`
found *"Current energy has more often been higher after a walk than without one. Higher
afterwards on 11 of 14 occasions with a walk and 4 of 14 without."* from readings alone, with
a comparison group, without asking him a causal question. That is the best thing in the app.
The failure is that it does not reach the recommendation (AUD-0027).

## G. Confidence, abstention and questions

**Question budget.** Six questions exist in total (`questions.ts:43`): sleep hours, energy,
usable time, child present, soreness, social energy. At most **3 per owner-local day**
(`guide.ts:49`). A question is asked only when its answers land somewhere different, measured
by re-running the whole pipeline under each answer — which is a genuinely good mechanism and
the reason the guide can ask zero.

**Does "no action" read as wisdom or failure?** Mostly wisdom. *"Nothing needs to move
tonight"* is well judged. But there are four distinct no-action states and two of them read
as failure: *"Nothing to suggest just yet"* (`nothing-proposed`) reads as the app not being
ready, and it is what a rested man gets at 07:00 and what a father gets on the three evenings
his daughter is away. See AUD-0034.

## H. Wording, explanation and interface

**Section 64 test, run properly.** Two materially different histories through the same
moment: one month of evidence vs nine months, same block, same friction. Result: **identical
recommendation, identical explanation, identical "why this one"**. Only an advisory line
differs, and it contradicts the explanation (AUD-0028). That is a section-64 failure with a
worked reproduction.

Run the other way — "Three broken nights" vs "The same week, properly slept", identical goal,
identical bad session, only sleep differing — the substance **does** change, correctly and
well. So the app is capable of substantive personalisation and fails it specifically where
the differentiating evidence is *learned* rather than *situational*.

**Does any wording shame, score, gamify or moralise?** Almost none, and this is a real
achievement. `owner-preference` explicitly refuses to read a refusal as "this does not work"
(`learning.ts:882-887`). The private domain is discreet. There is no score. The one exception
is `"3 times running"` about a child (AUD-0017).

**Private-domain discretion** (section 11) is handled correctly: off by default in exports,
gated behind a QA toggle, its own privacy class. The problem is the opposite one — it has no
influence at all (AUD-0011).

**Buttons and controls, audited.** The five lifecycle buttons are always drawn and disabled
rather than removed — that is **D-052**, a deliberate fix for a real double-tap defect, and it
is right. The Life page's per-domain refresh pair ("I've been keeping on top of this" /
"Something's changed") is a good concept. The QA laboratory is excellent. Findings below.

## I. Architecture — challenged, not assumed

**Is this architecture producing intelligence or constraining it?** Producing, on balance, and
the constraints are mostly the right ones. One `decide()`, one arbiter, an architecture-guard
test that fails the build if a feature reaches around it, evidence carrying provenance, four
knowledge states with no way to ask for a default, an append-only log with explicit
supersedes. These are the decisions that make the app trustworthy and none of them should be
loosened.

**Where does information exist and fail to reach the part that needs it?** Six named seams,
and every one of them produced a finding above:

1. `assembleSituation` reads nine of fifteen concepts by hand → four domains are write-only
   (AUD-0011).
2. `explain.ts` switches on the candidate's *trigger*, fixed at generation time, so it can
   only say why a move was proposed and never why it won (AUD-0027, AUD-0015b).
3. `arbitrate.ts` computes the margin into prose `notes` that only the trace reads
   (AUD-0033).
4. `findLimiter` is not passed the block that `assembleSituation` computes twelve lines later
   (AUD-0001).
5. `insights.ts` computes trajectories and life seasons that no decision can read (AUD-0029).
6. Learned beliefs reach the *score* and a standalone advisory line, never the *explanation*
   (AUD-0028).

Five of the six are one shape: **a value is computed correctly and the consumer that needs it
is upstream, or in another file with no channel.** That is a strong argument that the pipeline
decomposition is right and the *data flow through it is too narrow* — the fix is threading
richer structures between stages, not re-decomposing.

**Would a simpler architecture produce better intelligence?** No. The complexity is in the
right places and the largest files are coherent rather than accumulated — with one exception:
`insights.ts` at **2,500 lines** is doing card construction, rate computation, trajectory
analysis, life-season detection and copy, and AUD-0029 needs its analysis extracted anyway.

**Would a different decomposition serve better?** The pipeline (by stage) is right for a
single decision. What is missing is a *second axis* — by horizon — and AUD-0020's thread is
the minimal form of it. Decomposing by life-area would breach section 17.2 and D-030 and
should not be considered.

**Is the hybrid/advisor seat pulling its weight?** No, and this is documented rather than
hidden. See AUD-0039.

**Where do two subsystems hold contradictory beliefs?** Three, all found and filed:
AUD-0028 (learned band vs explanation), AUD-0037 (two sufficiency counters), AUD-0001
(situation says morning, limiter says tonight).

**What would I build differently, knowing what the ledger and the QA reports now show?** One
thing: the *horizon* would be a first-class parameter from the start — of the vocabulary, of
the freshness model, of the outcome model and of the comparison model — rather than a word
typed 113 times. Almost every P0 in this audit is a consequence of it not being one.

# 6. DO-NOT-CHANGE — things that look wrong but are right, and things that are working

This section is required and it is not a formality. Several of the items below looked like
defects during Stage 1 and turned out to be deliberate, reasoned and correct. Changing them
would be a regression.

**1. All five lifecycle buttons are always drawn, including "Done" before anything has
started, and "Start it" after a move is under way.** This looks like a state-machine bug and
is **D-052**, a fix for a real defect: removing a button re-flows the row, and a fast double
tap on "Start it" slid "Done" under a finger that had not lifted, recording a completion that
never happened — a legal transition, plausible, and indistinguishable downstream from the
truth. Leave it exactly as it is.

**2. Health & Physical Capacity and Sleep & Recovery share one page.** Eleven domains, ten
pages. This is **D-078** and the page says why on its own subtitle: "read together, because
the app already reads them together". It is correct.

**3. `hybrid` and `deterministic` produce identical recommendations.** This is not a broken
toggle. **D-024** records that both scored 60/60 on the ten golden profiles and chose the same
move on every one, and treats the agreement as evidence for the simpler architecture — which
is the correct reading of section 18's "simplest architecture that *clearly* produces better
decisions". AUD-0039 asks for a better rubric; it does not ask for the seat to be changed or
for inference to be wired in. **D-025 is an owner decision.**

**4. "Something else" is recorded as a decline.** It looks like the app conflating "show me
another" with "I don't want this". It does not: `episode.wantedAnother` is set and
`WANTED_ANOTHER_WEIGHT` down-weights it in `owner-preference` (`learning.ts:861`). This is
careful work.

**5. A refusal never reaches `immediate-benefit`.** `owner-preference` is the only dimension a
decline can influence, and the comment saying why is one of the best in the codebase: a
refusal is the owner exercising sovereignty, and reading it as a verdict on the move would
punish him for saying no. **Protect this rule structurally.**

**6. The association engine.** `MIN_PAIRS = 4` **on each side**, a required comparison group,
`MATERIAL_GAP = 0.34`, confounding kinds named and checked, per-context bands, an
`ActionFamily` pooling table that is deliberately **empty** and whose entries require a
written `because` enforced by an architecture guard. The resulting sentence — *"Current energy
has more often been higher after a walk than without one. Higher afterwards on 11 of 14
occasions with a walk and 4 of 14 without."* — is the best thing in the product: specific,
comparative, non-causal, and discovered rather than asked for. Do not loosen any of it.
AUD-0027 asks only that it be *shown* on Now, not that it be relaxed.

**7. Growth changes are proposed and never applied.** **D-070**. The owner confirms or
declines; nothing is written until he answers; both answers are records; it is a watershed
rather than a mute button. AUD-0017 asks to *amend* the criterion (setting spread alongside
occasion count) and explicitly does not ask to remove the proposal-not-application rule.

**8. Stale coverage is the weakest limiter and scores zero.** **D-063**. A quiet life area is
the app's own blind spot, not an obstacle in the owner's evening, and it is labelled "Out of
date" rather than "What is in the way" (**D-073**). This is a subtle, correct distinction that
took a defect to find. Leave it.

**9. Nothing is written when a screen renders.** **D-043**. AUD-0025 proposes a separate
session-scoped ledger precisely so this rule survives intact; it must not be "fixed" by
appending a recommendation record on render.

**10. The engine may name its own routines and never the owner's life.** **D-021**. A
candidate with no resolvable subject renders nothing (**D-018**). This is what prevents the
"it" problem and it is load-bearing.

**11. Timezone, local day, local week and DST handling.** Verified live across five zones,
three week-start settings and a DST boundary: local day and week held correctly, blocks
tracked the zone, and the weekly direction stayed pinned to the week it was set in. Nothing
in this audit touches it and nothing should.

**12. `nothing-worth-doing` as a real answer.** *"Nothing needs to move tonight"* on a Thursday
with 15 minutes free is right, well worded, and reached from a full picture. AUD-0034 splits
the *other* no-action reason and deliberately leaves this one alone.

**13. "Spend the next 30 minutes with Adaya, phone away."** Time *with* her is a first-class
move, separate from working *on* something with her. This is the single best product decision
in the fatherhood domain. AUD-0019 asks for it to vary; it must not be merged into
`growth-opportunity` or made conditional on a developmental purpose.

**14. Legacy concepts with no honest home are archived and named, never mapped to the nearest
fit.** **D-101**. Every one of the 28 families has a disposition and a reason, in two registers
— one for the audit trail and one for the owner. AUD-0030 asks that the *consequence* be
stated more plainly; it does not ask for a single family to be force-fitted.

**15. Entity identity for imported goals comes from the old record id, not from the
statement's words.** `translate.ts:604-632`. Two goals worded identically a year apart are two
goals. AUD-0038(c) proposes a *review-time question*, never automatic text-based merging.

**16. The QA laboratory itself.** The probe — facts with their state and purpose, every
candidate with its reason, what was ruled out and why, the full dimension breakdown with
notes, the learned beliefs with their evidence ids and provenance, and the counterfactual
"what would change the answer" panel — is an exceptional diagnostic surface and it is how
most of this audit was found. Do not simplify it.

# 7. Unresolved questions — the owner's to decide, not mine

**Q1. Should the app know Adaya's age, and hold a normative reference?**
*Options.* **(a) No age.** The app never risks framing normal development as a gap; it also
cannot tell an ordinary four-year-old from a real one, and every growth judgement stays
age-blind. **(b) Age plus a public normative reference** (e.g. CDC *Learn the Signs. Act
Early.*), used only to say what is age-typical and to refuse to propose skills that are not
yet typical — never to place her on a scale. **(c) Age only, no norms** — enough to compute
her age for wording, no developmental claims.
*What each implies.* (b) is the only option that lets the app say the most reassuring thing a
parenting product can say — *this is normal* — and it is the only one that puts a normative
statement about children into a record about his daughter. If (b), the rule must be written
into the decision log **before** any code: the app may say what is typical; it may never say
where she sits. **AUD-0018 is blocked on this.**

**Q2. Should section 61's target-style example be re-read?**
The plan's own illustration of good copy is *"This has worked several times in situations like
tonight."* AUD-0002 changes that word at ~30 call sites.
*Options.* **(a)** Read the example as illustrating *register* only, and make the horizon
block-aware everywhere. **(b)** Amend the example in the plan so the constitution and the code
agree. **(c)** Leave the word as canonical and accept the app sounds like an evening app.
*Implication:* (a) works but leaves the plan reading as though "tonight" were the target; (b)
is a plan edit, which the authority order says is the owner's. I recommend (b) alongside (a).

**Q3. Should D-036 be amended?**
AUD-0031 argues the share rule cannot represent the cost of being wrong and proposes a narrow
`consequential` exception for soreness and severe sleep shortfall.
*Options.* **(a)** Amend D-036 as proposed. **(b)** Leave D-036 and accept that the app will
recommend exertion without asking about pain. **(c)** Replace the share rule with a full
expected-opportunity-loss calculation.
*Implication:* (c) is the theoretically right answer and needs probabilities the app does not
have; (a) buys most of the benefit for two concepts and one flag. I recommend (a).

**Q4. Should legacy outcome and skill history become admissible evidence?**
AUD-0030(b). Today `execution`, `outcome`, `recommendation-effect-evaluation`, `skill-claim`
and `milestone-observation` are archived and inert.
*Options.* **(a)** Leave them inert — the brain starts cold and every belief is built from
this generation's own evidence, cleanly. **(b)** Admit them as *closed historical episodes* at
a distinctly lower reliability, visibly marked, reversible.
*Implication:* (a) is safe and throws away years of the owner's real life, including his
daughter's recorded development. (b) risks an old decision model's assumptions leaking into
the new one's beliefs. This is a judgement about how much he trusts the previous generation's
records, which only he can make.

**Q5. How much should the app be allowed to plan?**
AUD-0020 proposes threads — named courses of action over days or weeks.
*Options.* **(a)** No plans; the app answers "what now" and nothing else. **(b)** Threads, but
only ones the owner starts. **(c)** Threads the app may propose and the owner may stop.
*Implication:* (a) keeps the app small and permanently unable to run a certification, a
recovery week, or a growth ladder. (c) is what makes six separate findings go away and is the
change most likely to make the app feel like it is nagging if it is got wrong.

**Q6. Should live model inference be wired in?**
**D-025** already records this as requiring an owner decision, and nothing in this audit
changes that. Recorded here only so the answer is visible alongside the rest: AUD-0039
recommends improving the tournament rubric *whether or not* inference is ever wired in,
because the current rubric cannot detect the difference this audit is about.

**Q7. Should Now carry a second sentence?**
AUD-0022 (compatible moves) and AUD-0026 (the cost clause) each add a clause to the
recommendation. Together they could produce three clauses.
*Options.* **(a)** Hard cap at one appended clause of either kind, ever. **(b)** Allow two.
*Implication:* section 6 forbids Now becoming a feed of cards, and three clauses is that
failure arriving as prose. I recommend (a) and have written both findings assuming it.

# 8. Proposed Phase 8.5

**Name.** *The app is wrong about the time, and about his daughter.*

**Why a phase and not a backlog.** Nineteen items, one theme, one gate. They are the findings
where the app states something untrue on a screen the owner reads. Phase 9 will typeset those
screens and take them to a phone gate; anything not fixed first becomes settled design.

**Scope — in.** The nineteen items listed in section 4. **Scope — out.** Every structural
change (threads, commitment windows, registry-driven situation, the score re-cut, the four
dormant domains). Those are Phase 10, and starting one of them inside 8.5 would turn a
two-week correction into a re-architecture and delay Phase 9 for no visual benefit.

**Sequence** — Stages 0 to 4 of section 5, in order.

| Step | Items | Delivers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **8.5.0 The instrument** | AUD-0008 | Two morning scenarios and a one-press block sweep. Everything after this is verified with it, and the sweep is what proves the temporal class is actually gone. |
| **8.5.1 The horizon** | AUD-0002 → 0001, 0036; AUD-0005 | One shared vocabulary; the limiter, the buttons, the question options and the evidence panel stop asserting the evening; sleep stays valid for the day it describes. |
| **8.5.2 The morning has an answer** | AUD-0003 | A morning recovery verb, and the invariant that a named limiter always has a candidate in every block. The single most valuable fix in the audit. |
| **8.5.3 Adaya** | AUD-0014 → 0015(b), 0016; AUD-0037 | A decline stops counting as practice; the nonsense sentence goes; the reason for a growth opportunity stops being the age of the app's records; the two contradicting counters are reconciled. |
| **8.5.4 Honest sentences** | AUD-0032, 0028, 0027, 0026, 0033 | Inferences are spoken as inferences; the unfounded causal claim goes; the app's own best evidence reaches Now; trade-offs and near-ties are said out loud. |
| **8.5.5 Interaction** | AUD-0025(session), 0023, 0034, 0031 | No repeating itself within a day; declines escalate to a question and then stop; the situation line survives every no-action state; the guide asks about pain before prescribing exertion. |

**What Phase 8.5 delivers.** An app that is right about what time it is, right about what it
knows and does not know, right about what it has learned, and right about the owner's
daughter — verified block by block rather than evening by evening. No new domain, no new
structure, no new screen. In the language of the canonical acceptance sentence: the same
decisions, told truthfully, at every hour of the day.

**Gate.** The block sweep from 8.5.0 across all twenty scenarios at five blocks, plus the
independent QA protocol (D-077 / D-090 — QA runs in Codex, cold, and audits meaning before it
duplicates gates). Two specific acceptance items, both of which currently fail: **no
owner-visible string asserts the evening outside the evening**, and **a named limiter always
has a candidate that addresses it**.

**What Phase 8.5 explicitly does not deliver.** Strategy, plans, sequences, the four dormant
domains, Adaya's age, spaced study, cross-domain combination, or a re-cut score. All of those
are argued for above, all of them are Phase 10, and none of them is a reason to delay Phase 9.
