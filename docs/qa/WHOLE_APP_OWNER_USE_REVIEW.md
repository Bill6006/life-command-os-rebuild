## 1. Owner-level emotional and product reaction

This does not yet feel like my personal operating system. It feels like a careful, occasionally perceptive next-move engine attached to a record of my life.

There is real intelligence here. Protecting recovery after three broken nights instead of pushing study is the right kind of cross-domain decision. Remembering the exact subnetting difficulty is materially better than “work on your career.” Recognizing that Adaya has independently handled a particular skill in more than one setting, and asking whether it is settled instead of grading her, is thoughtful. The observed-versus-unobserved distinction in the walk evidence is unusually careful. These are not cosmetic achievements.

But the product understands repeated local events better than the life those events are supposed to build.

It can know I studied subnetting without knowing whether I am becoming employable as a cloud engineer. It can know I walked without knowing whether I am becoming stronger or visibly more muscular. It can know that I spent time with Adaya without adequately understanding the relationship, experiences, and support I want to provide. It can hold a faith-practice note without appearing to care about my desire for closeness to God or my uncertainty. It can know a domain exists while having almost no representation of my desired end state inside it.

The most deflating screens were not broken ones. They were honest, quiet screens with no convincing route from “I do not know” to “I am going to learn something important about you.”

“You have not mentioned these, and nothing is asking you to” is a particularly revealing sentence. It protects me from administrative homework, which is good. It also exposes a cold-start trap: the system can wait for me to know what matters, know how to enter it, and know which missing information would improve its decisions. That is a large part of the intelligence I wanted it to supply.

The “Can't now” interaction produces a similar feeling. I give the system a potentially valuable fact about the fit between its advice and my life; the move goes away. It may have recorded an inability, and the roadmap says that distinction affects feasibility. But as an owner, I did not see it learn what was in the way, preserve the intention, or find a version that could work. Silence can be respectful. Silence without learning can also feel like abandonment.

### Would I trust it?

I would provisionally trust several narrow tactical judgments when the relevant facts and evidence are visible. I would not yet trust it to steer my overall life. Its understanding of destination, capability, priorities, and strategy is too thin. A modest recommendation with an honest explanation is more trustworthy than a grand claim, but modesty alone does not fulfill the product promise.

### Would I keep opening it?

For a useful immediate suggestion, occasionally. For the feeling that something is actively helping me become the person I want to be, not reliably yet. The Life pages often answer “what was recorded recently?” rather than “what is happening here, where am I trying to go, and what is the next meaningful step?” Timeline offers memory without enough interpretation. Empty Insights gives little sense of an active inquiry. Repeated small moves and exhausted-candidate silence weaken the reason to return.

### Is it alive or passive?

Alive inside a few narrow loops; passive at the level of my life. It reacts to energy, sleep, a weak topic, a child opportunity, and some recorded outcomes. It is much less visibly curious about motivations, aspirations, resources, relationship context, recurring friction, and whether the overall strategy is working.

### Would six months, one year, or five years make it more valuable?

Six months could produce useful contextual micro-patterns. The long-history scenarios show that this is plausible: weekday versus weekend differences, a changed life season, declining usefulness of a familiar move, and measured state associations.

One year should also produce a richer understanding of what I can do, what I am building, which strategies fit my real life, and what the next frontier is. The current mechanisms and visible pathways do not establish that. More entries do not automatically become a better model of a person.

Five years risks producing a very long, conscientious diary around a small recommendation vocabulary. The missing element is not simply more history. It is a destination-and-strategy model that can use that history to change course.

### The central verdict

The foundation is worth keeping. The current product is not sufficient for the owner's long-range vision, and completion of the existing listed roadmap would still leave important gaps.

The required step is from:

> “Given these current facts and past responses, this familiar action looks reasonable.”

to:

> “I understand what you are trying to become, what progress would look like, what has been getting in the way, and why this next step is the best use of the life you actually have.”

That does not require a wall of recommendations, dozens of scores, constant questions, or false certainty. It requires better underlying questions, objects, evidence, and feedback loops.

## 2. Scope, evidence rules, and limitations

### 2.1 What was actually done

The normal product was experienced before QA scenarios. Now, all listed Life/domain surfaces, Timeline, Insights, More, manual inputs, recommendation controls, empty and populated states, and a 390 × 844 mobile-sized viewport were inspected. Buttons were clicked, facts were entered or corrected, recommendations were started/completed/declined, a short course was accepted/stopped, and several rich synthetic histories were examined.

The Preview explicitly states that its contents are invented. Synthetic test inputs included a cloud-engineering learning topic, restless sleep, enough energy, no current soreness, a half-hour window, Adaya present, an emotional-state sentence, and a faith-practice sentence expressing uncertainty. These were browser-preview interactions, not production-owner records. This review did change synthetic preview state through normal controls; it did not silently treat the session as read-only.

No backup restore, destructive reset, deletion of owner data, or real-world external action was performed. Data/export surfaces were inspected, not used to send private data to an external assistant. Mobile reflow was checked in the browser; this was not an owner-phone or physical-device test. The viewport was restored afterward.

### 2.2 How claims should be read

- **Observed:** directly visible browser behavior or wording from the sealed pass.
- **Interpretation:** the owner-level consequence of those observations.
- **Not found:** no discoverable route in the inspected surfaces; not proof that no internal data structure exists.
- **Roadmap-supported:** documentation describes a capability or intention. This is not independent verification that it is shipped correctly.
- **NEW GAP:** the reviewed roadmap does not specify the material capability needed here, or specifies only a narrower capability. A broad aspirational promise is not full delivery coverage.
- **Unverified anomaly:** an observed discontinuity whose cause was not isolated. It is not promoted to a confirmed data-loss or engine defect.

The review did not inspect implementation source to infer hidden behavior. Historical audit descriptions of source code are quoted or paraphrased only as roadmap context, not presented as new code verification.

The initial workspace contained Bills Planner, not the authoritative rebuild. It was excluded from the comparison. The correct repository is D:/Code/AI Coding Agents/Claude Code/life-command-os-rebuild. Legacy Life Command OS repositories were not treated as authority. The six roadmap documents listed in section 12 matched the file blobs in the reference checkout.

The browser and roadmap reference SHAs differ. This review does not assert bundle equivalence between them. Observed behavior belongs to the browser build above; roadmap classifications belong to the documented reference state. “More” still described Phase 8/legacy work and the next visual-coherence phase, while current authoritative status described Phase 82. That inconsistency is reported as orientation evidence, not a deployment diagnosis.

### 2.3 What remains unproved

No synthetic scenario proves real multi-year usefulness, clinical effectiveness, financial outcomes, relationship quality, or career readiness. No real week of natural owner behavior was observed. Delayed outcomes, tomorrow-boundary behavior, recovery after a long absence, device loss, cross-device continuity, and every lifecycle edge were not exhaustively tested.

Some actions caused surprising changes in the preview's displayed history. Shared preview/session state or fixture behavior may have contributed. Those observations are retained below with their uncertainty; no claim that real records were deleted is made.

## 3. Preserved sealed browser observations

This ledger preserves the browser evidence independently of later roadmap explanations. Scenario names identify synthetic histories, not claims about the owner's actual life.

### E01 — First contact and the invitation to return

Now initially said “Nothing worth starting right now” and, in another state, “Nothing here would bring anything back about it today.” An energy question was available. “Show mine” then said “Nothing to suggest just yet. There is plenty of history here, and none of it says how today is going. One answer below is usually enough.”

Owner reaction: honest about today's missing context, but the product's reason to ask was narrow. It wanted enough state to choose an action; it did not seem to want to understand the life I wanted.

### E02 — Energy unlocked the familiar walk

Answering that there was enough energy produced “Move for 25 minutes: a walk.” The reason referred to enough in the tank and the afternoon. Evidence showed current energy around 3/5, soreness 0/5, one comparable occasion, and little basis for effect learning.

The context dimensions shown included part of day, restedness, weekday, Adaya's presence, and roughly available time. Those are useful but limited. Place, equipment, preparation, actual physical program, desired adaptation, and the owner's available activity repertoire were not established.

A “Where this stands — Done” presentation also appeared against prior local preview history. That was retained as a confusing state observation, not a verified new lifecycle defect.

### E03 — The system can run out of things to say without learning much more

Later Now said “Nothing new for today. Everything this history has to suggest has already been in front of you today, and tomorrow starts again,” followed by “Nothing else worth asking right now.”

Adding a cloud-engineering topic, Adaya's presence, and a time window did not expose a destination-discovery conversation. This felt like exhaustion of the current candidate/question vocabulary, not a confident conclusion that my important needs were served.

### E04 — Life is organized around evidence freshness

Life explained itself as “Where you come to see what the app understands — never to keep it running.” Its groupings included Recent, Quiet, Going quiet, and Nothing here yet. For untouched domains it said “You have not mentioned these, and nothing is asking you to.”

The low-maintenance intention is sound. The resulting orientation is mostly about record recency, not outcome, direction, progress, or the next frontier. School/work-hours controls and, in suitable histories, “Things you have going” were present.

### E05 — Health and Sleep share a page but are distinct concepts

Health & Recovery exposed current energy and soreness/pain under Health, and hours slept plus sleep quality under Sleep. The shared page is not itself a defect. The problem is the depth of understanding: no visible model connected this material to strength, muscular development, physical capability, or a meaningful training progression.

Sleep-hour correction used coarse choices such as Under 5, About 6, Seven or so, and A full night. No exact-hours input was found in that flow. Coarse answers can reduce burden; they also limit precision when the owner actually knows the number.

### E06 — Fatherhood has one of the strongest specific progression examples

Sparse Fatherhood showed child-in-care/current-presence information and custody. Rich histories added a specific developmental skill and concrete occasions.

“Three times running” showed independent ordering of food on three occasions across more than one place and asked “Worth calling settled?” with “Yes, she's got this” and “Not yet.” The page described what she was working on, allowed the settled judgment to be reversed, and explained that settled skills stop being repeatedly suggested while remaining open to later checking.

The broader growth pass also showed a sequence in which success in familiar settings led to trying somewhere new before treating the skill as settled. This is a good model: actual occasions, context transfer, owner confirmation, no child score. It should not be confused with comprehensive fatherhood intelligence.

### E07 — Career knows a topic more readily than a destination

The sparse Career page offered Current learning topic and Usable time now. Entering “Cloud engineering (AWS)” stored a topic; no ordinary path surfaced a competency map, career destination, milestone sequence, prerequisite model, or “what should I learn next?” conversation.

Some rich scenarios had existing goals and pieces. Therefore “there are no goals” would be false. The issue is limited normal authoring/discovery and weak semantics of progress.

### E08 — Goals exist, but the owner-entry path was weak

A seeded Social goal, “Get easier with people I do not know,” offered Done, No longer this, and Date and pieces. In the inspected interaction, Date and pieces exposed a date field asking when the owner wanted it done; no add-piece or create-goal pathway was found there.

Existing Career pieces could appear elsewhere. This distinction matters: displaying fixture-supplied objects is not the same as making those objects discoverable and maintainable through normal use.

### E09 — Money was essentially a cash-buffer note

The inspected Money surface offered Cash buffer as free text. There was no visible target, unit-sensitive progress path, obligation-to-freedom linkage, or milestone model. A cash-buffer fact can be useful; it is not a financial-resilience or freedom system.

### E10 — Social, Emotional, Faith, Home, and Private were mostly thin fact surfaces

Social exposed social energy, with a seeded confidence-related goal in one history. Emotional Health exposed a single free-text Current emotional state. A test sentence could describe being confident, stressed, and lonely simultaneously, but no distinct dimensions or subsequent interpretation were visible.

Faith exposed Recent faith practice, described as the owner's own definition. The test input “Prayed for 10 minutes, but still felt uncertain” was stored as text. No visible mechanism connected practice, desired closeness, honest doubt, exploration, or later reflection.

Home exposed Home friction. Private exposed Recent private pattern and promised “Yours to enter. Nothing here appears anywhere else.” No explicit owner control for private-derived cross-domain learning versus inspect-only storage was found.

### E11 — Long-Range Direction was weekly direction

The page subtitle was “This week's focus, and what it is aimed at.” The actual editable concept was Weekly direction. No discoverable upstream model of who I am becoming, a multi-year destination, or a hierarchy connecting that direction to this week's choices appeared.

### E12 — Romantic life had no explicit home

Love/Dating/Romantic life was absent from the Life domain list. The registry's eleven domains appeared as ten pages because Health and Sleep share a page. Romance may overlap Social, Private, or Faith, but no visible representation explained how the owner's distinct romantic aspiration would be understood and steered.

### E13 — Manual capture accepted words without exposing their meaning

Several editable facts used unlabeled text inputs with Save and Cancel. No semantic confirmation showed whether an entry was understood as a current fact, persistent constraint, aspiration, preference, event, or uncertainty.

Correcting current facts was possible. An ordinary historical-event correction/retraction flow was not found on Timeline. Some domain navigation clicks were transient/no-op in the session; following observed route links reached the pages. This is not treated as proof of missing routes.

### E14 — Timeline is readable history, not yet useful long-term recall

Timeline described “Everything that happened, in order” and displayed chronological records. It had no visible search/filter/event-correction interaction in the inspected surface; a Show earlier affordance appeared in a longer pass.

The distinction between “everything that happened” and “everything recorded here” matters. The product does not observe the owner's whole life. Over years, a record list needs ways to answer meaningful questions without requiring the owner to perform the analysis.

### E15 — Empty Insights was honest but passive

Insights said “Nothing worth saying here yet” and explained that comparable occasions were needed. It also said “Nothing needs doing about this. It fills in through ordinary use — a suggestion taken, and an answer to what came of it.”

This avoids pressuring the owner. It does not explain how the system obtains missing comparison occasions, learns unmodeled states, or seeks high-value evidence when ordinary use cannot produce it.

### E16 — Data and review exports expose a manual bridge

More/Data offered export, backup, and restore. The interface explained local-browser storage and that data leaves through export/backup. A review export could be handed to an external assistant; private content was opt-in and off by default.

The sparse review export showed 16 beliefs, one proposed move, no entities, no active goals, and mostly operational unknowns. This was UI evidence, not source inspection. The external-review bridge is useful, but it should not be mistaken for an in-product strategy review loop.

### E17 — The near-empty QA history showed the curiosity boundary

“One answer, and a lot of silence” contained four records yet Now used “plenty of history” wording. The beliefs shown were: learning topic, usable time, weekly direction, emotional state, energy, recent faith practice, child here now, child in care today, custody arrangement, soreness/pain, home friction, cash buffer, private pattern, sleep quality, social energy, and hours slept.

The eight possible questions shown concerned learning topic, time, weekly direction, emotional state, energy, child care, soreness, and sleep quality. No destination, success criterion, foundational aspiration, available resources, or strategic obstacle question appeared.

This is a snapshot of the visible vocabulary, not a claim that sixteen concepts is a permanent implementation maximum.

### E18 — A short study course can carry continuity

“Two sessions in” suggested ten minutes recalling subnetting before reopening notes, identified the third of three sessions, and explained that it was the next step in something already underway.

Start it changed the recommendation to Under way and disabled the start control. Done settled the attempt and, in different tested histories, led to another recommendation or a no-action/energy-question state. No demonstrated mastery conclusion followed completion of the course.

The main pass found a Life overview “Things you have going” list with Stop this. A separate longer pass did not find the list on its inspected path. Positive evidence takes precedence: the list exists in suitable states. It was not consistently discoverable at the relevant domain, and no richer resumption or revision flow was found.

### E19 — Weak-topic specificity is real

“A topic keeps slipping” proposed going back over subnetting because /26 boundaries had been wrong twice the previous day. It could offer “Three sessions on subnetting” with “Yes, keep going,” after which Life showed the first of three and two remaining.

This is much better than generic study advice. However, the ordinary evidence panel emphasized topic and time rather than clearly exposing the specific failed-retrieval evidence driving the recommendation. The course supplied continuity without a visible standard for success or what comes after it.

### E20 — Recovery can appropriately override a deadline

After three broken nights, Now said “Take tonight as recovery — no subnetting session,” noting nine hours down across three nights. It could propose a three-night recovery course. A properly rested version of the situation instead recommended weak-topic review.

This is a strong positive: recovery is treated as a legitimate decision, not a failure of productivity. It does not establish that the system can determine which recovery strategy improves restorative sleep or waking energy over time.

### E21 — Weekly direction influences tradeoffs

In “A week pointed at the house,” a kitchen reset beat thirty minutes with Adaya because the weekly direction was a calmer house. The explanation explicitly named time away from that focus.

This makes direction consequential. It also raises a larger question the interface could not answer: which relationships/responsibilities are protected, how much maintenance is enough, and when a weekly focus should yield to a scarce or higher-horizon opportunity.

### E22 — “A month worked” showed evidence and a correction ambiguity

A kitchen reset was preferred to recall because it “Pays back more tomorrow.” Evidence showed four of four attempts happening and four of four owner judgments that it made a difference. Those judgments were labeled as what the owner had said.

“Not how it went” immediately removed the recommendation. In the ensuing preview state, Insights and other displayed material changed markedly, including a much smaller set centered on a CCNA goal. The interaction did not first explain correction scope or show an obvious undo at that moment.

This was not isolated into a reproducible real-data-loss defect. It is an observed trust/discoverability problem and an unverified state-discontinuity anomaly.

### E23 — Completing moves can immediately create more work

Completing the kitchen reset produced a recall suggestion; completing recall produced a walk. The walk could be preferred to a lab because it was “More likely to actually happen,” even where recent walks had made little difference.

Immediate recomputation is useful. An endless conveyor of feasible small tasks is not automatically a good life. The interaction did not clearly mark “enough for today,” close a meaningful milestone, or distinguish necessary maintenance from strategic advancement.

### E24 — Different refusal controls have different visible outcomes

Something else on the kitchen recommendation led to an Adaya recommendation and exposed a stop-suggesting-kitchen option. Not today also moved on; Timeline described the kitchen action as Passed / Turned down.

Can't right now on the Adaya recommendation made it disappear. Now became “Nothing needs to move tonight” with a sleep question. Timeline described the action as Not then / Did not fit at the time.

The wording does distinguish inability from refusal. What was not visible was a learned cause, an adapted version, a preserved next opportunity, or an explanation of how this particular constraint would improve the next decision. The sleep question was not an explicit answer to “what blocked time with Adaya?”

### E25 — Opportunities are sometimes concrete

Saturday with Adaya produced “Spend the next 30 minutes with Adaya, phone away,” justified by a window that would close. Saturday among people produced “Start one real conversation while at the climbing gym,” in a getting-out-more direction.

These are contextually grounded and specific. They are still mostly generic activities relative to the owner's goals of exceptional closeness, developmental opportunity, charisma, and social capability.

### E26 — Observed state associations are a real strength

“Two months of readings, and nothing graded” showed energy higher after walks on 11 of 14 occasions versus 4 of 14 without a walk. It also showed 67 energy readings around 2.7/5 and a soreness comparison around 1.6 versus 2.3.

The detailed evidence distinguished the exact action, required appropriately timed readings, excluded two confounded occasions, and excluded three unknown-exposure occasions rather than counting them as no-walk days. It explicitly described observation rather than causation and acknowledged selection bias. Correction was available.

This is evidence that the product can learn something without asking the owner to diagnose the cause. It is not evidence of broad multivariable or delayed-strategy discovery.

### E27 — Long histories can change the interpretation

A nine-month history showed walks looking less useful after May, with five of five earlier owner-positive results versus two of five later; kitchen resets differing on weekdays versus weekends, six of six versus two of six; a lab happening on two of six occasions; a changed custody/life season reducing the relevance of 39 earlier entries; a sister-related result with limited favorable evidence; and sleep readings around 7.6 versus 6.1 in a comparison.

These are contextual and longitudinal readings, not merely streaks. Historical explicit worth judgments remained labeled as judgments. No claim is made that the current live product asked a newly prohibited causal question simply because those old answers were displayed.

The gap is broader discovery: strong-week combinations, timing and lags, sequences, what changed together, what to investigate next, and whether a strategic adjustment worked.

### E28 — Goal “progress” was carefully described but limited

The CCNA insight described none of three pieces having had a session and a target about 41 weeks away. The named pieces included subnetting, VLAN, and OSPF. It clarified that a piece counts when a completed move or answered result touches it, and that this did not establish whether enough time remained.

That honesty should survive. Coverage of topics is not knowledge, retention, practical competence, or readiness for a cloud-engineering role. Avoiding an unsupported percentage is correct; avoiding a meaningful progress model is not.

### E29 — Mobile was not the main problem

At 390 × 844 the inspected screens reflowed readably without horizontal overflow. The bottom navigation was about 53 pixels tall, its buttons about 86 pixels wide, and action buttons wrapped. A screenshot confirmed the layout. This does not certify all accessibility or physical-device interactions.

The primary weakness was conceptual: even a beautifully arranged version of the current screens would still lack destination, strategy, curiosity, and constraint-learning depth.

### E30 — A second “Can't now” path ended the day's suggestions

The already-completed independent workflow pass used “Two months of readings, and nothing graded.” A walk supported by the observed energy association was on Now. Can't right now produced “Nothing new for today,” the explanation that everything this history could suggest had already been shown, and “Nothing else worth asking right now.”

Timeline recorded Not then / Did not fit at the time — getting out for a walk. Advancing the QA clock from 18:10 to 19:10 did not restore the suggestion or produce a blocker question, adaptation, or retained later intention.

This supports the owner's disappearance experience on a second action. It does not prove that inability is always suppressed for a whole day: cumulative shown/refusal rules and the narrow candidate set can produce this outcome. The later roadmap expressly distinguishes block-scoped inability from day-scoped decline. The product problem remains that the owner cannot tell what was learned or what will happen next.

### E31 — A historical completion appeared to govern today's action

On Show mine, Tuesday 25 August, a walk was presented as Done without Start/Done/decline controls. Timeline's matching walk suggestion/completion was on 22 August; today's entries were observations. Evidence identified 22 August as the one comparable occasion, while the review export still described a current walk suggestion and “Did a walk happen?” follow-up.

This is stronger evidence of a possible action-identity versus occurrence-identity mismatch than E02 alone. It remains a suspected bug requiring isolated reproduction, not a source-confirmed diagnosis.

### E32 — Completion did not visibly establish achievement

In the ordinary synthetic state, Enough energy produced a walk; About 6 hours of sleep produced a 1.5-hour-short reading without removing the walk. Start it showed Under way. Done produced Nothing new for today. No result question appeared immediately or after leaving and returning.

On the weak-subnetting history, Done did not ask whether /26 boundaries were now correct, what remained difficult, or what had been retained. Advancing by an hour and a day did not produce a specific follow-up in that pass. However, the QA Timeline later used a generic “Followed through on a suggestion here” event, so subject/history preservation was uncertain. This is evidence of a missing demonstrated owner loop, not proof that all delayed outcome paths fail.

### E33 — History can contain corrections that the normal surface cannot author

Existing Timeline history included “Withdrew an earlier entry — That was about yesterday, not today” and “Hours slept last night: 6.75 hours — replaced an earlier entry.” The underlying history vocabulary is therefore richer than the currently discoverable editing surface.

No ordinary Timeline route was found to correct a date or subject, backfill an event, retract accidental completion, or add missing context. Changing energy from 3 to 2 through Not right? left both readings in Recently without a visible superseded marker. A retained audit trail is good; an unexplained pair of readings leaves ambiguity about what the engine currently believes.

### E34 — Alternatives and stopped courses exposed additional preview ambiguities

A sparse-history recommendation named subnetting recall as chosen over a walk. Something else returned to “Nothing to suggest just yet,” said today's state was unknown, and asked energy despite a recent Enough answer. This could be QA fact-preservation behavior rather than a production reset.

After stopping a three-session course, Life displayed two identically titled entries, one “Ran out of time on its own” and one “Stopped.” The course really is visible/stoppable; the ambiguity is whether these are distinct courses, duplicate fixture history, or an unclear lifecycle presentation. No internal cause was inferred.

### E35 — Deferral and schedule support exist, with limits

“Before the house is up,” at 05:30, said “The morning suits Adaya better than now” and explained that the morning had room for thirty minutes with her while the early morning did not. Only evidence was available, with no retained-intention or window-control affordance.

At 09:30 the same scenario recognized school until 15:00 and recommended a walk. The review skipped intermediate times, so it cannot claim the deferred opportunity was never offered. The supported problem is that “morning” did not give the owner a sufficiently precise, inspectable commitment to return to.

School/work capture handled start/end and weekdays versus every day. Irregular days, exceptions, travel/setup time, and a broader set of competing commitments were not visible. Positively, another school-morning state shortened fatherhood time to ten minutes before school and recognized school attendance later. This is genuine adaptation, not a universally fixed thirty-minute recommendation.

### E36 — Privacy protects content more clearly than metadata

Private text was concealed in Timeline as “Private entry,” not displayed in full. That is a useful protection. It still revealed that a private entry existed and when it occurred, which is narrower than “Nothing here appears anywhere else” can imply. Export excluded private content by default and explained the omission.

The workflow pass's screenshots failed and it did not complete mobile QA; the main pass later obtained and inspected the mobile screenshot recorded in E29. These are distinct evidence scopes, not a contradiction.

## 6. Product findings

### Reading the finding register

P0 means a foundational gap in delivering the stated personal-OS purpose, not a verified outage or security emergency. P1 materially impairs daily trust, learning, or long-range steering. P2 is important usability, reach, or interpretability work. P3 is minor polish. No confirmed catastrophic data-loss or security defect was found.

Types use the requested vocabulary. A finding may cross disciplines; one primary type is assigned. “Phase 9 structure: yes” means the visual phase should account for the owner-facing object/state/relationship. It does not authorize implementing everything before Phase 9 or silently expanding Phase 82.

Source keys [C], [A], [D], [S], [L], and [N] resolve in section 12. Coverage references describe the authoritative plans, not findings retroactively derived from them.

Timeline uses different wording, and [D] D-045 explicitly separates inability/follow-through, decline/appetite, and completed-result/effect. D-119 further distinguishes a block-scoped inability from a day-scoped decline. This is important work to preserve.

The critique is more precise:

> The system records that the move did not fit, but did not visibly learn what made it unfit, preserve the useful intention, or use that friction to improve the strategy.

That is not solved by a different status label or a lower feasibility estimate.

In both sealed inability paths, the move disappeared. One left a sleep question; another left no question and said tomorrow starts again. The second remained silent an hour later. Those observations support the owner's experience, but do not prove the internal rule is “discard for the entire day.” Refusal escalation, shown limits, available candidates, and known context can combine to produce the same visible result.

The critical product question remains:

> If this system is supposed to care about steering my life, why would it discard one of the most valuable pieces of constraint information I just gave it?

It need not interrogate the owner to answer that question. It must use the signal intelligently.

### 7.2 Meanings that must remain distinct

| Owner meaning | What may reasonably change | What must not be inferred automatically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I cannot do this in the current time window | Current feasibility, duration, possible later opportunity | I dislike the action, it does not work, or I lack commitment |
| I am in the wrong place / lack equipment or privacy | Context/resource prerequisites | The goal is unimportant |
| I am with Adaya or working | Responsibility compatibility and opportunity timing | The responsibility is an obstacle to eliminate or the action is rejected forever |
| I am too tired, sore, or in pain | Current suitability; perhaps stop or recovery | A smaller exertion is always safe or appropriate |
| Something unexpected interrupted me | Current attempt state and remaining work | Nothing was completed or the strategy is ineffective |
| I do not want this recommendation | Preference for this action/occasion, perhaps an alternative | Permanent veto or objective ineffectiveness |
| I do not want this suggested again | Explicit, scoped, reversible preference | Deletion of the domain, goal, or historical evidence |
| This recommendation was based on a wrong fact | Correct the fact and reconsider | All historical outcomes for this action were false |
| This approach keeps failing to fit my real life | A strategy/setup review | The owner is unreliable or the goal should be abandoned |
| I do not want to explain | Respect quiet; retain the scoped inability as unknown cause | A fabricated reason |

### 7.3 When a tiny follow-up is worth asking

Ask only if the answer has a credible path to a better decision or valuable future understanding. The criterion is not “a reason would be interesting.”

Good triggers include:

- The current recommendation could be adapted in materially different ways depending on the blocker.
- A high-priority goal is repeatedly blocked and the cause remains unknown.
- A known time/place/resource assumption may be wrong.
- One answer could identify a recurring constraint affecting several recommendations.
- A safety-relevant unknown would determine whether to offer an easier move or stop.
- The owner has signaled willingness to clarify.

Poor triggers include:

- The app already knows the relevant constraint and it is still current.
- The owner just answered, skipped, or asked to be left alone.
- There is no useful adaptation, decision, or future-learning consequence.
- The next action is an obvious respectful stop.
- The owner is in a time-pressured or interrupting context.
- The only purpose is to fill a database field or satisfy an outcome quota.

A useful interaction might be one compact optional question such as “What's in the way?” with a few context-relevant choices and a skip path. It should not present every possible blocker after every tap. It should not demand “why are you avoiding this?” or convert a constraint into a psychological diagnosis.

Sometimes no question is the intelligent response: “You're working until six; I'll leave this for now.” That is acceptable only if the working window is actually known. Sometimes the best response is simply to stop, with no claim about a later opportunity.

### 7.4 What learning should follow

The capability needs the meaning of:

**Intention and action → actual context → inability cause, if known → scope/expiry/confidence → response chosen → what later happened → updated understanding.**

This is a semantic requirement, not a prescribed schema or implementation.

The system should distinguish a one-off disruption from a recurring constraint, a fact the owner stated from an inference, and a known later window from a guess. The owner must be able to correct a persistent assumption. Uncertainty should survive an unanswered question.

### 7.5 Response options, not an automatic cascade

| Situation | Useful response | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not enough time; a genuinely useful smaller action exists | Offer the smaller version or preserve a tiny entry step | Do not pretend five minutes produces the same outcome as the original action |
| Wrong environment or missing resource | Offer a compatible activity or preparation step if worthwhile | Do not substitute irrelevant busywork just to keep a recommendation visible |
| With Adaya | Recognize responsibility; consider a genuinely compatible shared activity | Do not instrumentalize every moment with her or assume compatibility |
| Tired/sore/in pain | Respect the limitation; stop, recover, or ask a consequential concrete question | Do not automatically reduce intensity and assume that solves the problem |
| Known better window | Defer and carry the intention into that window | Do not invent bedtime, free evenings, equipment access, or future capacity |
| Unknown future availability | Leave the intention inspectable, or ask one useful scheduling fact | Do not label a guessed time “better” |
| Repeated same blocker | Review setup, routine, or strategy | Do not keep lowering feasibility until the important action disappears forever |
| Owner wants no engagement | Quietly respect it | No interrogation, guilt, or “you failed” framing |

### 7.6 Pattern-level learning is the larger prize

Examples are hypotheses the product could investigate, not facts established about this owner:

- Labs are repeatedly proposed during fragmented windows; the strategy may need a protected setup-ready opportunity.
- A particular exercise requires equipment that is unavailable on custody days; another real routine may serve the same physical aim.
- Social opportunities repeatedly collide with fatigue after work; the system may need a different context rather than more exhortation.
- A home-maintenance task repeatedly consumes the only learning window; a durable environment change may be more valuable than another reset.

The app should learn the fit between action and life, not a moral judgment about compliance.

### 7.7 What the roadmap already supplies—and what it does not

Already supplied or planned:

- Distinct inability, decline, and effect semantics: [D] D-045.
- A block-level response to repeated refusals and an optional existing-vocabulary question: [A] AUD-0023; [D] D-119/D-125.
- Repetition suppression without promoting harmful or unsuitable runner-ups: [D] D-124/D-126.

Still missing or incomplete:

- Blocker-specific adaptation and a carried intention.
- Recurring constraint memory with expiry, provenance, and owner correction.
- Shared-constraint discovery across several actions.

Therefore F07/F08 remain product/intelligence findings even though the existing roadmap already takes inability seriously. They are not dismissed as “covered by the Can't now button.”

## 8. Failure, interruption, completion, and recovery

The review found a useful beginning of a lifecycle, not a complete model of living through a plan.

| Life event | Demonstrated support | Important missing distinction or unproved path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recommendation shown | One primary recommendation and evidence | Exposure is not action or life evidence; durable suppression is not demonstrated across all contexts |
| Start | Under way state; active recommendation continuity | Wrong-context or long-unsettled attempts; meaningful partial state |
| Finish a session | Done event and recomputation | Attempt completed versus capability gained versus later outcome |
| Interrupt | Can't now/other controls can end the current presentation | Started-and-partly-done, interruption reason, remaining work, resume |
| Decline | Different wording and a next alternative in some states | Temporary preference versus refusal of the goal/strategy |
| Can't now | Distinct “did not fit” event | Cause, adaptation, intended later opportunity, recurrent blocker |
| Stop a course | Life overview Stop this | Optional reason, pause/revise/resume, undo, duplicate-title ambiguity |
| Course expires | Expired wording exists | Owner reorientation and distinction from intentional abandonment |
| Correct a conclusion | Suppress learned assumption; history retained by documented design | Scope, alternatives to broad suppression, visible reversal |
| Correct an event | Existing history can contain corrections | Discoverable ordinary authoring/backfill and superseded markers |
| Return after a lapse | Staleness/life-season mechanisms exist | Gentle restart without old assumptions or catch-up pressure |
| Complete a milestone | Goal Done exists | Evidence-supported closure, next frontier, changed strategy |
| Reach a better future window | Hold can name the next block | Inspectable pending intention, missed opportunity, changed prerequisites |

Two principles follow.

First, **failure to execute and failure of strategy are different**. A strategy cannot be fairly judged ineffective if it was never feasible enough to try. Conversely, faithfully completing its actions is not proof that it is working.

Second, **recovery should preserve dignity and useful information**. A missed week does not require a scolding review. An interrupted action does not require a questionnaire. A one-tap stop can be appropriate, provided the owner understands what stopped and can recover from an accidental choice.

The system also needs action-appropriate semantics. “Done” on a kitchen reset can mean an observable room condition; “Done” on a study session means time/attempt, not mastery; “Done” on recovery cannot prove restoration; “Done” on fatherhood time cannot establish closeness. These are not reasons to make all flows longer. They are reasons to ask the right concrete question at the right time, or to wait for evidence.

## 9. The eight special-question audit

### 1. How does the app progressively get to know me?

**Current answer:** Primarily by collecting current facts, recording responses to a limited action repertoire, receiving outcomes, and accepting manual domain edits. Some history becomes specific learned evidence. Rich scenarios show that the model can preserve named people, topics, places, and a life season.

**Judgment:** Partial and too reactive. The visible question vocabulary mostly serves immediate decision readiness. Untouched domains can remain untouched indefinitely. The ordinary owner journey does not yet demonstrate how aspirations, resources, constraints, important relationships, and success criteria enter the model.

**Needed:** A low-burden longitudinal discovery agenda, including foundational direction and high-information follow-ups, separate from—but coordinated with—today's guide. New knowledge should visibly affect future choices. See F01/F02/F04/F19.

### 2. How does it discover patterns I cannot identify myself?

**Current answer:** The strongest example compares appropriately timed energy readings with and without a specific walk, excludes confounded and unknown-exposure cases, and states the limitations. Other examples identify context splits, drift, temporal reversal, and changed life seasons.

**Judgment:** A credible foundation, not yet a broad hidden-pattern discovery system. The app demonstrated several local relationships, not the combinations and sequences behind the owner's best/worst periods. Sparse ordinary capture also limits what could ever be discovered.

**Needed:** Wider concrete observations, active evidence acquisition, cautious hypothesis generation, lags/context interactions, counterexamples, competing explanations, and strategic consequences. Never a literal “100% Peak State” or proof of causation from association. See F15–F18/F27.

### 3. How does it turn multi-year desires into daily execution?

**Current answer:** Weekly direction affects ranking; some goals have dates/pieces; short courses connect several actions; commitment windows constrain today.

**Judgment:** It supplies fragments of the chain, not the chain. A statement/date/pieces list is not a destination/baseline/milestone/strategy model. “This topic had a session” is not career readiness. Long-Range Direction mostly exposes this week.

**Needed:** Explicit, revisable connections from desired life outcome to milestones, strategy, next priority, action, and progress evidence. Cross-horizon tradeoffs must protect responsibilities and avoid permanent maintenance-mode. See F01/F03/F05/F06/F14/F20–F29.

### 4. How does it know whether its strategies are actually working?

**Current answer:** It learns about action feasibility, some owner-rated worth, observed state associations, and trends. It can count a short course's sessions.

**Judgment:** It does not visibly evaluate strategies as strategies. Completion, fit, and effectiveness are not yet joined to the destination. A three-session course can finish without a demonstrated result or changed next step.

**Needed:** Strategy-specific expected evidence and review triggers; distinguish not tried, repeatedly infeasible, insufficient evidence, and tried without desired progress. Adapt the setup or strategy and then evaluate the adjustment. See F03/F05/F08/F11/F18.

### 5. How does it prevent repetition from making the system feel stupid?

**Current answer:** The product has refusal/shown suppression, reversible veto support, some child-skill settlement, and context-sensitive recommendations. Documentation explicitly prioritizes diverse owner routines.

**Judgment:** Suppression is necessary but insufficient. It can replace repetitive advice with exhausted silence while leaving the owner's real needs unmodeled. Repeating a useful routine is fine; repeating the only modeled action is not.

**Needed:** A richer action repertoire with distinct demand/outcomes, purpose-aware repetition, progression, context fit, and an explanation when a familiar action remains best. Do not manufacture novelty. See F12/F13/F14/F24/F42.

### 6. How does it decide what new information is worth asking me for?

**Current answer:** The guide favors information that materially changes the immediate recommendation. The roadmap includes consequential-question exceptions, refusal-triggered reconsideration, and fixes to materiality declarations.

**Judgment:** Good for today's decision, incomplete for long-term knowledge. A destination question or recurring-blocker question can be extremely valuable even when it would not alter today's top-ranked action.

**Needed:** Consider decision consequence, future information value, recurrence, uncertainty, owner effort, sensitivity, freshness, and whether the fact is already known. The question must have a use; “could store it” is not enough. A skip should be respected. See F02/F07/F17/F30/F44.

### 7. How does it recognize that my life or priorities have changed?

**Current answer:** Facts can be corrected, goals can end, weekly direction can change, evidence can become stale, and a custody change can create a new season that reduces older evidence's relevance.

**Judgment:** This is one of the stronger foundations. It still relies heavily on a change becoming represented in the right place. It did not demonstrate a gentle reorientation when patterns shift, a strategy stops fitting, or the owner returns after an absence.

**Needed:** Detect candidate changes cautiously, ask a useful confirmation, revise the relevant goals/constraints/strategies, and preserve the earlier season rather than rewriting it. See F08/F19/F31/F32/F34.

### 8. After one year, what does it know that it could not know in week one?

**Plausibly, from demonstrated mechanisms:** Which of its familiar actions are more feasible in certain contexts; limited observed state associations; whether owner judgments changed over time; weekday/weekend differences; a specific child skill's repeated occasions and transfer; some stale assumptions and season changes.

**Not established by the current ordinary-use pathways:** Whether the owner is materially stronger, employable as a cloud engineer, financially freer, exceptionally close to Adaya, more socially capable, closer to God, or following a strategy that actually fits their life.

**Judgment:** A year can improve calibration inside the current model without sufficiently expanding the model. The product must earn a richer answer than “more confidence about walks and kitchen resets.”

**Needed:** Year-one knowledge should include destinations and revisions, capability gains, working/failed strategies, recurring constraints, meaningful relationships/opportunities, and cautious discoveries the owner could not have made alone. See F01–F05/F15–F19/F31/F44.

### 11.6 Important blind spots not dependent on the owner's explicit prompts

**The closed-world evidence trap.** A system can improve its estimates of the actions it already knows while never discovering the activities that matter most. More history is not automatically broader understanding. F04/F12/F17 address this.

**The feasibility trap.** If a hard but necessary activity rarely happens, simply lowering its rank can optimize away the owner's ambition. The valuable inference may be “change the setup,” not “recommend something easier forever.” F08/F14.

**The completion proxy trap.** Counting sessions/topics can make progress look legible while leaving competence unchanged. No percentages is not enough protection; semantic clarity is required. F05/F22.

**One action identity, many occurrences.** Stable identity is necessary for learning, while separate instances are necessary for lifecycle truth. Confusing them can make a new recommendation inherit an old completion. This is a suspected observed bug, not just a theoretical concern. F43.

**Success can be a bad stopping rule.** Immediately proposing another task after every Done can undermine enoughness, recovery, and meaningful closure. F11.

**“Later” can be a disappearing intention.** A correct broad block is not yet a dependable owner experience if the intended action cannot be inspected, missed windows are silent, and changed context is unexplained. F09.

**Privacy metadata and inferred influence matter.** Hiding the raw sentence does not hide that a private event occurred. And a future private-informed recommendation could reveal its premise indirectly. F30.

**Correctability needs a grammar.** “That didn't happen,” “that happened yesterday,” “that no longer applies,” and “I disagree with the inference” should not all be the same destructive-feeling gesture. F32.

**A good fixture can conceal an acquisition failure.** The product must show how a real owner creates the evidence and entities that make its strongest demonstrations possible. F04/F38.

**Preserved history is not necessarily usable memory.** The roadmap's legacy-import tradeoff explicitly archives some old outcomes and skill evidence without admitting them to learning. That was discovered during document comparison, not the sealed browser pass. The cost must be explained; automatically treating old semantics as current truth would be equally wrong.

**Quantification and scoring people are not the same thing.** Rejecting a Life Score or a child-development grade should not prevent honest measurements of strength, cash buffer, retained knowledge, or progress toward an owner-defined target. Conversely, a numeric activity count is not an outcome just because it is easy to calculate.

**The system can optimize engagement with itself.** More taps, more completed suggestions, and more records can look successful while the owner's life is not improving. F44.

**A deliberately passive domain can still violate the product purpose.** Faith is the clearest example. Respectful uncertainty should lead to careful owner-directed curiosity, not permanent non-participation.

### 11.7 What should be protected

Do not lose these strengths while addressing the gaps:

- Usually one primary move, not a feed of competing advice.
- Permission to rest, stop, defer, or say “I do not know.”
- Recovery overriding a study push when the evidence warrants it.
- Named people, places, topics, actions, and specific weak points.
- Distinct inability, dislike, explicit veto, and observed result.
- No automatic causal claim from an owner's favorable opinion.
- Unknown exposure excluded from the no-action comparison group.
- Confounding/counterexamples/provenance and contextual limits in evidence.
- Adaya's concrete occasions and context transfer, owner-confirmed settlement, reversibility, and no child grade.
- Discreet private content and export exclusion by default.
- Low-burden inspection/control rather than compulsory domain maintenance.
- Correctable interpretations over preserved history.
- Readable mobile reflow.
- No artificial novelty, composite Life Score, or manufactured certainty.

### 11.8 Acceptance questions for the product vision

These are research/critique criteria, not an authorized implementation checklist.

Before calling the product a life-steering system, it should be able to demonstrate an ordinary-use journey in which:

1. It learns a meaningful desired outcome the owner did not pre-model for it.
2. It helps make the next milestone concrete without requiring a fully specified plan.
3. It distinguishes action completion from evidence of actual progress.
4. It learns a recurring blocker and changes the approach while preserving the goal.
5. It asks fewer redundant questions because previously learned context is used.
6. It discovers a cautiously supported relationship the owner did not identify first.
7. A discovered pattern changes a real decision or strategy and is later reevaluated.
8. A life change or interruption leads to humane revision, not obsolete advice or blame.
9. The owner can correct meaning and control privacy without breaking trust in the memory.
10. After months, it can show not just more activity but what the owner is becoming, what remains uncertain, and what the next progression step is.

This review does not require all of those capabilities to be implemented before visual work begins. It does require recognizing that the current roadmap's finite packages do not collectively guarantee them.

### 12.3 Final disposition

**Review complete. Product purpose not yet fulfilled.**

The app is not empty of intelligence. It has useful local judgment, a careful evidence foundation, and several owner-respecting design choices. Those strengths make the larger gaps more important, not less.

The highest-leverage change is to organize the product around what the owner is trying to become, then make discovery, constraint learning, evidence, and strategy revision serve that direction. A more attractive arrangement of the current fact surfaces will not accomplish that by itself.

The next product discussion should decide which of these newly identified capabilities belong in the intended product contract and which structural relationships Phase 9 must accommodate. This document does not alter the roadmap, approve a phase, select unresolved owner policies, or authorize fixes.

Only this review artifact was created. No product code was changed, no fixes were implemented, and the sealed browser pass was not repeated to manufacture additional evidence.
